Physical Profile

Size Range
Adult males reach 15 to 20 feet in length; females are slightly smaller.
Weight
350 to 550 lbs.
Identification Markings
Extremely long, thin snout filled with interlocking teeth. Adult males develop a bulbous cartilaginous growth on the tip of their snout called a "ghara." [2]

Habitat & Geography

Gharials are highly aquatic and prefer the calm areas of deep, fast-flowing rivers in Northern India and Nepal.

Ecology & Behavior

Feeding and Diet

Gharials are specialized piscivores (fish-eaters). Their diet includes:

  • Small to medium-sized river fish
  • Occasional frogs and crustaceans (mostly eaten by juveniles)

Life History Cycle

  1. Nesting: Females dig steep hole nests in river sandbanks and lay 20-90 eggs.
  2. Hatching: After 70 days, hatchlings emerge. Unlike other crocodilians, mother gharials cannot carry babies in their mouths due to their needle-like teeth.
  3. Maturity: Juveniles stay in creches protected by adults. Males grow their signature 'ghara' upon reaching sexual maturity at around 10 years old. [3]

Communication & Adaptations

The male's "ghara" acts as a vocal resonator to create buzzing and bubbling noises used during courtship and territory defense. Their thin snouts allow them to slice through the water rapidly to catch fish.
